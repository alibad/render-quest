
# Positioning review — and the README that was advertising two labs

Asked where Render Quest stands as a resource for learning graphics interactively.
Nine agents surveyed the field live; a tenth was told to attack the flattering
conclusions. It found three things worth recording.

**The README still described the site as it was on day one.** It listed two labs,
said "two more are in progress: coordinate spaces, and light & normals" — both of
which shipped a week ago — and advertised "32 numeric checks" against an actual 69
unit checks and 101 render checks. The repo is the artifact a graphics person
actually lands on, and it was understating the project five-fold. Rewritten.

**The central claim of the last two weeks was wrong.** "Direct manipulation of the
pipeline is an unoccupied position" appeared in six of seven survey reports and does
not survive contact with LearnWebGL (learnwebgl.brown37.net) — a ~93-lesson
interactive WebGL textbook by C. Wayne Brown, live since 2016, whose primary teaching
device is exactly slider-driven demos of transforms, cameras, projections and lights.
It even has the camera-as-an-object-beside-its-own-view diagram that Lab 2 was
claimed to be first at; so does webglfundamentals. The honest version is that the
position is occupied by something dated and culturally invisible, which is a far
narrower claim than nobody-does-this.

**The gap is prose, not features.** Measured two ways: ~190 words of connective
explanation per lab by one count, 372 words of all rendered text per lab by another.
Either way the whole ten-lab sequence carries less writing than one Ciechanowski
article, and LearnOpenGL spends more words on coordinate systems alone than this site
spends on everything. The arguments are real and correctly ordered — the depth lab's
five presets are a five-beat case about frustums and sorting — but they exist as
button labels with the connective tissue unwritten. Nobody links to a button, Google
cannot index a slider, and a professor cannot assign a canvas.

## Key decisions

- **Recording the refutation rather than quietly dropping the claim.** "Nobody else
  does this" shaped a fortnight of work and turns out to be false; a silently edited
  belief teaches nothing and invites the same reasoning again.
- **Not adding an eleventh lab.** Coverage is ~24% of LearnOpenGL's core pages; a
  lab on framebuffers takes that to 27% and changes nothing about whether anyone
  arrives. The next unit of work is words.
