# The first lab becomes an essay

The positioning review's verdict was that the gap is prose, not features — ~190
words of connective explanation per lab, against LearnOpenGL's several thousand
on a single chapter. Arguments that were real and correctly ordered existed as
preset labels with the sentences between them unwritten. This is the prototype
of the fix, on lab 1.

## What we built

**A form: prose with single-control figures.** Two new pieces. `Prose` sets a
readable measure and typography for essay text. `Figure` is a canvas, at most
one control, and a caption written after the fact — if it needs two controls it
is two figures. The instrument at the foot of the page still has all sixteen
controls and answers every question at once, which is exactly why it cannot
teach any single one.

**Lab 1 rewritten as an essay**, 391 words to 1,472, with five figures:
translation moving one column, rotation leaving the last column alone, the
basis vectors as literally the first three columns, a non-uniform scale, and
the two composition orders side by side driven by one slider. Five linkable
section headings, which is the point — a search engine cannot index a slider
and a lecturer cannot assign a canvas.

Every figure drives the lab's own `createScene`, so a figure cannot drift away
from the instrument below it; they are the same renderer with different controls
exposed.

## What we checked

The order figure's claim is arithmetic, not assertion: T·R leaves the cube at
(2.20, 0, 0) — turned where it stood, then moved — and R·T at (1.26, 0, −1.80),
swung around the origin. Verified against the matrix library before the sentence
describing it was written.

## What we fixed

Headings rendered accent-blue, because the `[&_a]:link-accent` rule in `Prose`
was catching the anchor each heading carries for its own `#` affordance. Scoped
to paragraphs and lists.

## What we fixed, after looking at it

The essay column was centred inside the page's `max-w-6xl` main while the lab's
own title is left-aligned, so the prose started 234px to the right of the h1
above it and read as a different page pasted in. Left-aligned to match the
header. Only visible by opening the page — the build, the tests and the smoke
suite were all green with it.

## Key decisions

- **One lab first, not all ten.** The form is a bet — prose interleaved with
  isolated figures, in Ciechanowski's grammar. Better to see it working on the
  entry point than to write 15,000 words in a shape nobody has looked at.
- **Reusing the lab's scene rather than writing figure-sized ones.** A figure
  that renders differently from the instrument it introduces would be worse than
  no figure.

# The other nine labs become essays

The exemplar was lab 1. This is the remaining nine, written in parallel and then
put through a single editing pass for voice, repetition and factual accuracy.

## What we built

**Nine essays, 3,729 words to 20,360 across the ten labs.** Every lab now opens
with 1,400-2,400 words of argument, four to six linkable headings, and figures
that isolate one variable each, with the full instrument at the foot of the page
where it belongs.

Two shapes, by necessity. The six WebGL labs get live figures driving the lab's
own scene factory, so a figure cannot drift from the instrument it introduces.
The three that own their own device — compute, instancing, shader — get SVG
diagrams instead: requesting four WebGPU adapters to illustrate one page would
cost more than the figures are worth, and on a machine without WebGPU every one
of them would render a failure card in the middle of the prose. The instancing
diagram, which shows `instances[instanceIndex + objectRef.index]` with the two
terms colour-coded and a toggle for which side does the counting, teaches that
idea better than a canvas would have.

## What the editing pass caught

Nine writers working blind duplicated each other. The perspective divide was
argued from scratch twice, orthographic w = 1 near-verbatim twice, the six ±w
clip comparisons twice, and six essays reproduced the exemplar's closing
paragraph almost word for word. Each idea now lives in the lab whose subject it
is and is a half-sentence link everywhere else.

More usefully, it checked the numbers and found claims the source does not
support — a specular range quoted from a sub-range of the slider, a Gouraud
preset asserting the highlight is "smaller than a triangle" when the measured
half-angle is 6.15° against 5.6° spacing, a preset note that conflated two
different boxes, and a comment reading "10 floats" where the loop writes eleven.
Those were corrected in the labs, not just in the essays.

It also found a ban-list word in the exemplar I wrote.

## What we fixed

**/labs/instancing scrolled sideways on a phone**, by two pixels. The cause was
`device.limits.minUniformBufferOffsetAlignment` — a 44-character identifier in
inline code, wider than the prose column with nowhere to break. Inline code now
takes `overflow-wrap: anywhere` rather than `break-word`, because only the
former reduces an element's min-content width.

Worth recording how long that took to find: an SVG `<text>` reporting
`scrollWidth` of 394 looked like the obvious culprit and was a red herring — SVG
children report those properties in viewBox units, not CSS pixels, and the
element's real box was 36px wide and entirely on screen. Two fixes went in
against that false lead before hiding elements one at a time found the actual
one.

## Key decisions

- **Deciding the form rather than asking.** One control per figure, figures at
  prose width, claim before the figure and caption after. Three questions I had
  put to Ali across two turns; they were mine to answer.
- **Letting the editor edit the labs.** It changed preset notes and control
  labels that the source contradicted, which was past its brief. Kept: each was
  a one-clause correction of something demonstrably wrong.

# Closing the loose ends

The editing pass left four things it deliberately did not touch, on the grounds
that a human should decide. Three were bugs and are fixed; one was not a bug.

## What we fixed

**The depth lab predicted a fight where nothing fights.** `willFight` was
`separation <= resolvableGap`, which is true at exactly zero — and at exactly
zero the two panels have identical depth, the test is `LEQUAL`, and the panel
drawn second passes and wins every pixel cleanly. Stable, not shimmering. It is
the one setting where a smaller gap is *more* stable than a larger one, and the
readout now says "coplanar — later wins" rather than predicting a tear.

**The two transparency presets showed nothing wrong.** From the lab's opening
camera the three panes are already back to front, so "Transparency, drawn wrong"
looked identical to "drawn right". Both presets now carry the camera angle that
makes the failure visible, and the wrong one says it is showing you the panes
from behind.

**The shader lab had no way back.** Its preset effect was keyed on the preset
name, so clicking the preset you were already on did nothing — edit "A circle"
into something broken and the one button that should restore it was the one
click the effect ignored. Keyed on a counter now.

Verified by driving them: separation 0 / 0.002 / 0.2 report coplanar / will
fight / resolvable; the blend preset puts `azimuth=2.5` in the URL; and editing
the shader then re-clicking its preset restores the original source exactly.

## What we did not fix

**The shading lab's normal hairs are drawn through the model matrix, and that is
correct.** They are meant to show the naive normals being wrong — squashed with
the surface, leaning towards the equator when the surface needs them leaning
away. A separate figure carries the `correctNormals` toggle and shows what the
shading does about it. The essay already describes exactly this. "Fixing" the
overlay would delete the demonstration.

## What we updated

The essays made two pieces of copy stale within a day of being written. The labs
index still said "one idea per lab, with the controls to it", which describes
what a lab was before it had 2,000 words in front of the controls; and the README
described each lab as a canvas. Both now say what a lab is.

## Key decisions

- **Trimming the essay rather than keeping the workaround.** The depth essay's
  closing paragraph existed to warn readers about two bugs. With the bugs gone it
  is one honest fact about `LEQUAL` instead of two apologies.
